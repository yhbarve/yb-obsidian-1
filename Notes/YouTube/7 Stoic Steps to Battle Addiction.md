# 7 Stoic Steps to Battle Addiction
### By: Ryan Holiday
#### Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MTE6jEbR094
---
### 1. Recognize the Problem
- Take a step back and see what you're doing under the influence of an addiction.
- It could be drinking, eating junk food, smoking, etc. All of these things are bad for you and you know it.
- Don't be under the influence of these addictions. These things should not have a control over your life.
- The core question is **What temptations or urges are you going to allow to control you in your life?**
### 2. Pause and Reflect
- Ask yourself **How's this decision going to age?**
- Stoics tend to be less immediate. Think about the consequences of every action.
- Be in command of yourself. Be the master of yourself.
### 3. Don't let other things control you
- We give this power over to something else - alcohol, food, cigarettes, people, etc.
- Be in control of yourself.
- Alexander the Great was powerful but always wanted more. So according to the Stoic philosophy, he was poor.
- **More is all you need** is a hellish place to be in.
### 4. Discipline Equals Freedom
- If you're not disciplined, it means that something else is in command of you.
- To become what you're meant to become, you have to be disciplined.
### 5. Become more useful
- We are how we choose to respond to outside situations.
- Nothing is neither good nor bad, it just is. Time will tell if it was good or bad.
- You can just focus on giving your best, and most things will end up being positive.
### 6. Persist & Resist
- The secret to life is these two words.
- Keep doing things that are really hard, and stop doing some things even if it's tough to do so.
- This is basically self-discipline.
- It's deterministic and predictable - it'll make you better.
### 7. Stack up your wins
- You can read all the self-help books, and have all the knowledge in the world.
- But you still have to do something about it and get to work.
- It's just one day at a time.
- Momentum is real. Inertia of motion is real.
