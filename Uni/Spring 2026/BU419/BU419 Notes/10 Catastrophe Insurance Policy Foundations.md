# Why Catastrophe Insurance is Different
- Catastrophe losses are increasing worldwide. This isn't just a Canadian problem. 
- Governments around the world are facing the same challenge of financing increasingly costly disasters.
## Drivers of Increasing Catastrophe Losses
1. **Hazard**
	- Increasing frequency and severity of secondary perils
	- Climate change alters the frequency, severity, and spatial distribution of weather-related perils.
	- Leads to more intense storms, prolonged wildfire seasons, and heavier precipitation events
	- Also leads to compounding events such as storm plus flooding or wildfire plus wind
2. **Vulnerability**
	- The replacement cost of structures, infrastructure, and technology escalates with time and as real wealth grows.
	- Most regions have inadequate flood and wildfire mitigation strategies.
	- With the rise of technology, new risk features like solar panels and batteries have been created.
3. **Exposure**
	- Population growth in high-risk areas creates denser pockets of value, turning what would have been a localized natural event into a multi-billion-dollar catastrophe.
	- It also means that there are simply more high-value physical assets sitting in harm's way.
4. **Socio-economic effects**
	- Post-disaster surges in demand for labour and raw materials, combined with general economic inflation, significantly push up the repair and rebuilding cost.
## Why catastrophe risks challenge private insurance markets?
1. Losses are highly correlated
	- A single event can generate thousands of claims simultaneously.

2. Loss distributions are fat-tailed
3. Extreme losses require large capital
