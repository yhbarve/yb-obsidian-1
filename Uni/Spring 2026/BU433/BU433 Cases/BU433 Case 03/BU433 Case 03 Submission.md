**Q1. What problem is Upstart trying to solve?**
- Upstart's core mission is to promote financial inclusion by addressing the limitations of the traditional lending system.
	- Only 45% of Americans have access to bank-quality credit, despite the fact that 83% of Americans have never actually defaulted on a loan.
	- Because conventional models turn away these consumers, underserved individuals are forced to turn to highly expensive alternative lenders to secure capital.
- Key systemic frictions introduced by the traditional credit model:
	- Roughly 26 million Americans lack a credit score. Conventional systems automatically lock these individuals out of mainstream credit markets
	- Traditional models struggle to evaluate soft information, such as employment stability or future income potential based on education and career trajectory.
	- Information asymmetry between the borrower and the lender can result in adverse selection.
- - -
**Q2. Why does alternative data help to mitigate information frictions?**
- Upstart's models use borrower financial information from credit reporting agencies and augment this with alternative data provided by borrowers, such as education, cost of living, and loan application interactions.
- These variables do not measure the same thing as a FICO score. Instead, they focus on different aspects of the 5Cs, like the **capacity** to pay, and the customer's **character**.
- Factors like educational background and employment stability have a proven, strong correlation with a borrower's underlying financial stability and future trajectory. This makes them highly predictive of default risk, even if the individual lacks a deep credit history.
- Upstart’s internal data and independent academic research show that its risk grades provide significant incremental information over traditional scores.
- - -
**Q3. Is the use of alternative data fair?**
- Yes, because the use of alternative data democratizes credit by validating "invisible" borrowers who lack credit histories.
- On the other hand, alternative data can serve as an illegal proxy for protected characteristics like race or socioeconomic status. This can result in systemic disparities.
- Under fair-lending laws, disparities are permissible if justified by a "business necessity", provided no less discriminatory alternative exists.
