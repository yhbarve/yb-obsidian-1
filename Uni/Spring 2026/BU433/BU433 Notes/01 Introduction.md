### Financial Markets
- Financial markets are structures through which funds flow.
- Can be distinguished along two major dimensions:
	- Primary vs Secondary markets
		- Primary markets are those in which users of funds raise funds through new issues of financial instruments such as stocks and bonds.
		- Secondary markets are those that trade financial instruments once they are issued.
	- Money vs Capital markets
		- Money markets trade debt securities or instruments with maturities of one year or less.
			- T-Bills
		- Capital markets trade debt and equity instruments with maturities of more than one year.
			- Bonds & Stocks
- Financial instruments are subject to regulations imposed by agencies such as the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC).
---
### Financial Institutions
- Financial institutions perform the essential function of channelling funds from those with surplus to those with shortage.
- Types of financial institutions:
	- Commercial Banks
	- Thrifts
	- Insurance Companies
	- Securities Firms & Investment Banks
	- Finance Companies
	- Investment Funds
	- Pension Funds
	- FinTechs
- Financial institutions allow small investors to group their funds together by holding the claims issued by a financial institution. This minimizes the monitoring costs that a supplier of funds would have to face.
- Financial institutions act as asset transformers. They also diversify away some but not all of the investment risks.
- Some of the risks faced by financial institutions:
	- Default Risk
	- Foreign Exchange Risk
	- Interest Rate Risk
	- Market Risk
	- Off-balance Risk
	- Liquidity Risk
	- Technology & Operational Risk
	- Insolvency Risk
---
### Enterprise Risk Management
- Recognizes the importance of managing the combined impact of the full spectrum of risks as an interrelated risk portfolio.
- It is a process to embed risk management as a component in all critical decisions throughout a financial institution.
