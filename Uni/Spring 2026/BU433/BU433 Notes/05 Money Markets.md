### Money Markets
- Money markets trade debt securities on instruments with maturities of less than one year.
- Primary markets are where the securities are born.
- Secondary markets are where investors trade already issued securities amongst themselves before they mature.
- Money markets exist to meet the needs of individuals, corporations, and governments who have either a shortage of cash or an excess amount of cash.
	- For individuals with a shortage of cash, it serves as a source for a quick safe loan for a short period of time.
	- On the other hand for individuals with surplus cash, it serves as a source to invest money for a short period of time and earn a tiny bit of interest.
- Money market instruments share basic characteristics:
	- Generally sold in large denominations - $1M to $10M blocks
	- Low default risk
	- Maturity must be less than one year

### Yields on Money Market Securities
- Bond Equivalent Yield: $$i_{be} = \frac{(P_f - P_0)}{P_0}*\frac{365}{n}$$
	- $P_f$ = Face Value
	- $P_0$ = Purchase Price
	- $n$ = Number of days until maturity
- Bond Equivalent Yield can be converted to an Effective Annual Interest Return: $$EAR = \lparen1 + \frac{i_{be}}{\frac{365}{n}} \rparen^{\frac{365}{n}} - 1$$
- Some money market instruments use a 360-day year. $$i_d = \frac{P_f - P_0}{P_f} * \frac{360}{n}$$
- We can convert $i_d$ to $i_{be}$: $$i_{be} = i_d * \frac{P_f}{P_0}*\frac{365}{360}$$
- Some instruments pay interest only once during their lives. $$i_{be} = i_{sp}*\frac{365}{360}$$

### Types of Money Market Instruments
#### Treasury Bills (T-Bills)
- T-bills are short-term debt obligations issued by the US government to cover government budget deficits and to refinance maturing government debt.
- Original maturities are 4, 13, 26, and 52 weeks and T-bills are issued in denominations of multiples of $100.
- These are issued through a formal process called the Treasury Bill Auction, in which the US Treasury sells new issues of T-Bills.
	- Competitive bids specify the amount of par value of bids desired and the discount yield.
	- Non-competitive bids, on the other hand, specify only the desired amount of face value and are limited to 5 million for a single bidder.
- Once issued the bills are traded in the secondary market, which is the largest for T-bills of any U.S. money market security.
#### Federal Funds
- Fed funds are short-term funds transferred between financial institutions, usually for a period of one day.
- Fed funds rate is the interest rate for borrowing fed funds.
- Primary risk is that the borrowing institution does not have to pledge collateral for the funds it receives, which are usually in the millions, so there's a tiny default risk.
#### Repurchase Agreements
- This is an improvement to the fed funds.
- A repurchase agreement involves the sale of securities by one party to another with a promise to repurchase the securities at a specific price on a specific date.
- So basically party A that needs funds will borrow them from party B in exchange for certain securities and if party A defaults, party B can simply sell the securities and recover their money back.
	- Usually the securities that are given to Party B are valued at a slightly lower rate than the market rate. This difference is called the haircut.
#### Commercial Paper
- Commercial paper is an unsecured short-term promissory note issued by a company to raise short-term cash.
- Because it's unsecured, there is no collateral against the borrowed funds. The system depends entirely on the credit rating of the firm that is raising the cash.
- To bypass the risk, institutions created asset-backed commercial paper, which secured the cash against bundles of other financial assets like mortgages, to convince the investors that it is safe.
- Yields on commercial paper are quoted on a discount basis.
#### Negotiable Certificates of Deposit
- A negotiable certificate of deposit (CD) is a bank-issued fixed-maturity interest-bearing time deposit that specifies an interest rate and maturity date and is negotiable.
- Whoever holds the CD when it matures receives the principal and interest.
- It can be traded any number of times in the secondary market.
#### Banker's Acceptance
- This is a time draft payable to a seller of goods with payment guaranteed by a bank.
- Usually arise from international trade transactions.
- BAs can be traded in secondary markets.

### International Aspects of Money Markets
#### Euro Money Markets
- Euro-dollar market is the market in which Euro-dollars trade.
- A eurodollar is a U.S. dollar-denominated deposit held in a commercial bank located outside of the United States.
	- Because foreign banks don't have to comply with expensive U.S. reserve regulations, they have lower operating costs.
	- This allows them to pay a higher interest rate on eurodollar CDs than what you would get on a standard CD inside the U.S.
- The rate offered for sale on euro-dollar funds is known as the LIBOR or London Interbank Offered Rate.
- So if party A lends funds to party B, party B will have to return the original amount plus an extra interest, which is calculated using LIBOR. $$Interest = Original\ Amount + (Original\ Amount * LIBOR * \frac{\# of\ days\ money\ was\ kept}{360})$$
- Euro dollar certificates of deposit and Euro dollar commercial papers also exist and work similarly to the concepts discussed earlier.
