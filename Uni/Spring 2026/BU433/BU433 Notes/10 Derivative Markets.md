# Ch 10: Derivative Security Markets
---
### Forwards & Futures
- A derivative security is an agreement between two parties to exchange a standard quantity of an asset at a pre-determined price at a specified date in the future.
- As the value of the underlying security to be exchanged changes, the value of the derivative security also changes.
- Historically there have been two types of contracts: spot & forward contract.
	- A spot contract is an agreement to transact involving the **immediate** exchange of assets and funds.
	- A forward contract is an agreement to transact involving the **future** exchange of a **set** amount of assets at a **set price**.
#### Futures Contracts
- A futures contract is very similar to a forward contract but is settled daily.
- Most trading takes place in trading pits using an open-outcry auction method among exchange members:
	- Floor brokers place trades from the public while professional traders trade for their own account.
	- Position traders take a position based on their expectations about the future direction of the prices of the underlying assets.
	- Day traders generally take a position within a day and liquidate before the day's end.
	- Scalpers take positions for very short periods of time, like minutes or hours.
- A futures trade order may be for a long position (the purchase of a futures contract) or short position (the sale of a futures contract).
- Clearinghouse is the unit that oversees trading on the exchange and guarantees all trades made by the exchange traders. It is basically the middle man that insures that the trading is being done in a fair manner.
#### Margin Requirements on Futures Contracts
- All parties are required to maintain an initial margin any time they request to trade.
- If losses on the customer's position occur and the level of the funds in their margin account drops below a stated level (maintenance margin), the customer receives a margin call.
- On receiving a margin call, the customer is required to deposit more funds into the margin account to bring the account funds back to the initial margin.

### Options
- An option is a contract that gives the holder the right but not the obligation to buy or sell the underlying asset at a specified price within a specified period of time.
	- A call option gives the holder the right but not the obligation to buy the underlying security.
	- A put option gives the holder the right but not the obligation to sell the underlying security.
	- The writer or seller of an option has the obligation to fulfill the trade if the holder decides to exercise the option. In exchange the holder pays a premium to the writer when buying the option.
- For a call option holder the loss is capped at the premium value, but the profit has no upper bound.
- For a call option writer, the profit is capped at the premium value but the loss has no upper bound.
- For a put option holder the loss is capped at the premium value and the profit is capped at the price of the option minus the premium.
- For a put option writer the profit is capped at the premium value and the loss is capped at the price of the option minus the premium.
- Options are valued using the Black-Scholes pricing model. this model examines five factors that affect the price of an option:
	1. The spot price of the underlying asset
	2. The exercise price of the option
	3. The option's exercise date
	4. The price volatility of the underlying asset
	5. The risk-free rate of interest
- An **American option** can be exercised at any time before and on the expiration date whereas a **European option** can be exercised only on the expiration date.

### Swaps
- A swap is an agreement between two parties to exchange a series of cash flows for a specific period of time at a specified interval.
- An interest rate swap is an exchange of fixed interest payments for floating interest payments by two counter parties.
	- The swap buyer makes the fixed-rate payments and the seller makes the floating-rate payments.
- A currency swap is used to hedge against exchange rate risk from mismatched currencies on assets and liabilities.
- Credit default swaps were developed to better allow financial institutions to hedge their credit risk.
- Swaps are not standardized contracts and swap markets were historically governed by very little regulation. The call for stricter regulation over these securities was strong in late 2008 and early 2009 because of the role they played in the financial crisis.
---
### Caps, Floors, and Collars
- A cap is a call option on interest rates, often with multiple exercise dates.
- A floor is a put option on interest rates, often with multiple exercise dates.
- A collar is a position taken simultaneously in a cap and a floor.
- Note: Caps, floors and collars are used only for interest rates and not on regular assets so basically you want to control your interest rates.
