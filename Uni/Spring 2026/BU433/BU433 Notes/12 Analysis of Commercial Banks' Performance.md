# Why evaluate the performance of commercial banks?
- Commercial banks are unique in the special services they perform and the level of regulatory attention they receive.
- The ultimate measure of a CB's performance is the value of its common equity to its shareholders.
- Financial statements of commercial banks are ideal candidates to use in examining the performance of depository institutions.

# CAMELS Ratings
- Regulators use CAMELS ratings to evaluate the safety and soundness of banks.
- Components:
	- **C: Capital Adequacy**
		- Evaluated in relation to the volume of risk assets
		- The volume of marginal and inferior quality assets
		- The bank's growth experience, plan, and prospects
		- The strength of management
	- **A: Asset Quality**
		- Evaluated by the level distribution and severity of adversely classified assets
		- The level and distribution of non-accrual and reduced-rate assets
		- The adequacy of the allowance for loan losses
		- And management's demonstrated ability to administer and collect problem credits
	- **M: Management**
		- Evaluated against virtually all factors considered necessary to operate the bank within accepted banking practices and in a safe and sound manner
	- **E: Earnings**
		- Evaluated with respect to their ability to cover losses and provide adequate capital protection
		- Trends
		- Peer Group Comparisons
		- The quality and composition of net income
		- The degree of reliance on interest-sensitive funds
	- **L: Liquidity**
		- Evaluated in relation to the volatility of deposits
		- The frequency and level of borrowings
		- The use of Brokered Deposits
		- Technical competence
		- Availability of assets readily convertible into cash
		- Access to money markets or other ready sources of funds
	- **S: Sensitivity to market risk**
		- Reflects the degree to which changes in interest rates, foreign exchange rates, etc., can adversely affect an FI's earnings or economic capital
- CAMELS ratings range from 1 to 5:
	- **Composite 1:** Institutions in this group are basically sound in every respect.
	- **Composite 2:** Institutions in this group are fundamentally sound but may reflect modest weaknesses correctable in the normal course of business.
	- **Composite 3:** Institutions in this group exhibit financial operational compliance weaknesses ranging from moderately severe to unsatisfactory.
	- **Composite 4:** Institutions in this group have an immoderate volume of serious financial weaknesses or a combination of other conditions that are unsatisfactory.
	- **Composite 5:** This category is reserved for institutions that have an extremely high immediate or near-term probability of failure.

# Financial Statements
- Federal Financial Institutions Examination Council prescribes uniform principal standards and report forms for depository institutions.
- Financial information on CB's is reported in two basic documents:
	1. **Report of condition or balance sheet** presents financial information on a bank's assets, liabilities, and equity capital.
	2. **Report of income or income statement** presents major categories of revenues and expenses and the net profit for a bank over a period of time.
- - -
# Assets
1. Cash and Due from Depository Institutions
	- Consists of vault cash, deposits at the Fed, deposits at other financial institutions, and cash items in the process of collection.
	- None of these generates much income for the bank.
2. Investment Securities
	- This consists of:
		- Federal Funds Sold
		- Repurchase Agreements
		- U.S. Treasury and Agency securities
		- Securities issued by States and political subdivisions
		- Mortgage-backed securities
		- Other debt and equity securities
	- These generate some income for the bank.
	- They are highly liquid with low default risk and can usually be traded in secondary markets.
3. Loans and leases
	- These are categorized as:
		- Commercial and industrial loans
		- Loans secured by real estate
		- Individual or consumer loans
		- Other loans
	- Major asset items on the bank's balance sheet and generate the largest flow of revenue income.
	- Least liquid asset items and a major source of credit and liquidity risk for most banks.
4. Other assets
	- This consists of items such as:
		- Trading assets
		- Premises and fixed assets
		- Other real estate owned
		- Intangible assets
		- Others
	- Generally a small part of the bank's overall assets
