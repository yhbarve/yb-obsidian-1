# Ch 15: Insurance Companies
---
### Insurance
- A device whereby an individual or a business transfers the risk of uncertain financial loss by payment of a premium.
- Broadly grouped as life insurance and property and casualty insurance, with further classifications:
	- Life insurance
	- Health insurance
	- Property insurance
	- Liability insurance
	- Credit insurance
	- Farm insurance
	- Reinsurance
#### Major Issues
- Adverse selection occurs when the people most likely to benefit from a transaction are the ones who actively seek out the transaction and are thus likely to be selected.
	- Insured members have higher risk than non-insured members.
	- Therefore, the loss probability statistics may not truly reflect the loss potential for the people who actually want to buy policies.
- Moral hazard refers to the fact that an insurance policy holder changes their behavior after being insured.
	- A generous automobile insurance policy may encourage reckless driving.
	- Concern arises when we cannot observe people's actions and so cannot judge whether a poor outcome is intentional or just a result of bad luck.
- to avoid these issues, insurance companies use the following strategies:
	1. Information collection and screening
	2. Risk-based premium
	3. Restrictive provisions
	4. Prevention of fraud through investigations
	5. Cancellation of insurance
	6. Deductibles
	7. Co-insurance
	8. Limits on insurance coverage
- Other strategies include:
	- The insured and the beneficiary must be related.
		- Insured is the party covered by insurance.
		- Beneficiary is the party receiving payout if loss occurs.
	- The insurer must be provided with complete and accurate information by the insured.
	- The insured is not supposed to profit from buying insurance.
	- The payout needs to be adjusted with any compensation from a third party.
	- The loss must be quantifiable.
- Insurance is usually sold through agents.
	- Independent agents sell insurance for a number of different companies
	- Exclusive agents sell insurance for just one company
#### Industry Structure
- In mutual insurance companies, accumulated profits are owned by policyholders.
- In stock insurance companies accumulated profits are paid out to shareholders as stock dividends, but the company is owned by outside shareholders who bought stock in the company.

### Life Insurance Companies
#### Overview
- Life insurance products protect nearly 29 million Canadians.
- The industry is competitive, with 100 Canadian insurers and 38 foreign-owned insurers.
#### Lines of Life Insurance
- Ordinary life insurance is sold to individuals.
	- Term life policy is pure life insurance with an expiry date, no savings element attached.
		- Temporary insurance protection
		- Low cost
		- No cash value
		- Usually renewable
		- Likely convertible to permanent life insurance
	-  Permanent Life Policy offers a package of pure life insurance protection and investing opportunities with no expiry.
		- Lifelong protection needs 
		- Have an investment component 
		- No expiry date as long as premiums are paid.
		- More expensive to own?
		- Build a cash value 
		- Loans are permitted against the policy 
		- It is further divided into whole life and universal life insurances.
			- Whole life insurance is the traditional form where the insurer takes on all risks related to death and the underlying investment performance.
			- Universal life insurance is where policyholders select investment options, and the insurer assumes the risk related to death.
	- Group life insurance covers a large number of people under a single policy.
		- Contributory policies involve both the employer and the employee sharing the premium costs.
		- Non-contributory policies are paid entirely by the employer
#### Assets & Liabilities
- With a steady flow of premiums and predictable benefit payments, life insurers can securely invest in long-term capital markets
- Liabilities come from the premiums paid by insured clients and from pension funds under management.

### Property & Casualty Insurance
#### Overview
- There are nearly 200 companies selling P&C insurance in Canada, with about half being foreign firms.
- **Property Insurance** 
	- Protects against the loss of real or personal property from events like fire, theft, or storms.
	- Policies are either **named-peril** (covering only specific listed events like floods) or **open-peril** (covering all risks except specific exclusions like war).
- **Casualty (Liability) Insurance:**
	- Protects against legal responsibility for damage caused to another person's property or an injury to another individual.
- In practice, property and casualty are often bundled together. The single largest line of business is **Automobile Insurance**, making up roughly 44% of net written premiums and claim costs.
#### Lines of P&C Insurance
- Insurance companies earn revenues through premiums and on investments they make.
- Premiums are reported in two ways:
	- Direct Written Premiums: Total premiums collected
	- Net Written Premiums: Total amount adjusted for portions paid to reinsurers
- To spread the risk, a re-insurance company agrees to accept risks of other insurance companies in exchange for a payment.
#### Assets
- Bonds
- Stocks
- Mortgages
- Real Estates
- Term Deposits
- Others
#### Liabilities
- About 57% of the total liabilities and equity are unpaid claims and adjustment expense items.
- Risk exposure of property insurance is limited to the property value.
- To determine insurance premiums, insurers consider the likelihood of a customer making a claim and how much those claims will likely cost.
	- Underwriting risk is the risk that premiums are insufficient to cover losses and operating expenses after taking into account investment income
	- Three sources of underwriting risk:
		- Unexpected increase in loss rates 
		- Unexpected increase in expenses 
		- Unexpected decrease in investment yields
#### Four Key Ratios
1. Loss Ratio = Actual claims incurred relative to the net premium earned 
2. Expense Ratio = Expenses incurred relative to the net premium written
3. Combined Ratio = Loss Ratio + Expense Ratio
4. Operating Ratio measures overall profitability by subtracting the investment yield from the combined ratio
	- Investment yield is measured as the net interest income divided by premiums earned
#### Regulation
- Federal and provincial governments share jurisdiction
	- OSFI governs through the Insurance Companies Act
	- Provincial regulations ensure the marketing of insurance products and licensing requirements
	- Canadian Life and Health Insurance Compensation Corporation is an industry-funded organization to protect policyholders.
		- $200,000 on claims in case of bankruptcy
		- $60,000 in cash policy coverage
#### Major Issues
- Insurance industry becoming more global
- Insurance crime
- Severe weather claims
- Catastrophic losses

### InsurTech
- Insurers are increasingly focused on consumers’ needs with more tailored product offerings.
- Companies continue to innovate and adapt through the following:
	- Artificial Intelligence
	- Robotic Process Automation
	- Machine Learning
- Many are beginning to pivot from investments that support business as usual to financing innovations that facilitate more fundamental business model changes.
