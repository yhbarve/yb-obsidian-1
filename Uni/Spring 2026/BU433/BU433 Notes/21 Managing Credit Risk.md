# Credit Risk Management
- Financial institutions are special because of their ability to efficiently transform financial claims of household savers into claims issued to corporations, individuals, and governments.
- This ability to process and evaluate information and control and monitor borrowers allows them to transform these claims at the lowest possible cost to all parties.
- Credit risk management is important for FI managers because it involves the determination of several features of a loan or debt instrument.
- Credit quality of many FIs' lending and investment decisions has attracted a great deal of attention over the past three decades.
- Managerial efficiency and credit risk management strategies directly affect the return and risks of the loan portfolio.
- One advantage that FIs have over individual investors is the ability to diversify some credit risk by exploiting the law of large numbers.
- A credit quality problem can cause an FI to become insolvent in the worst case, or it can result in such a significant drain on earnings and net worth that it can adversely affect the FI's profitability and ability to compete.

# Credit Analysis: Real Estate Lending
- Residential mortgage loan applications are among the most standardized of all credit applications.
- 2 considerations dominate an FI's decision to approve a mortgage loan application:
	1. Applicant's ability and willingness to make timely interest and principal repayments
	2. Value of Borrower's Collateral
- GDS and TDS are two ratios useful in determining a customer's ability to maintain mortgage payments.
## Debt Service Ratio (GDS)
- This is equal to the total accommodation expenses divided by gross income.
- Acceptable threshold is generally set around a maximum of 25% to 30%.
## Total Debt Service Ratio (TDS)
- This is equal to the total accommodation expenses plus all other debt service payments divided by gross income.
- Acceptable threshold generally is set around a maximum of 35% to 40%.

# Credit Scoring Systems
- FIs use credit scoring systems to calculate the probability of default or to sort borrowers into different default risk classes.
- Credit scoring systems are mathematical models that use observed characteristics of the loan applicant to calculate a score that represents the applicant's probability of default.
- Lenders may use standard FICO credit scores.
- FIs also verify borrowers' financial statements.

# Credit Analysis: Real Estate Lending
- Perfecting collateral is the process of ensuring that collateral used to secure a loan is free and clear to the lender should the borrower default on the loan.
- FIs do not desire to become involved in loans that are likely to go into default.
- In the event of default, lenders usually have recourse.
- **Foreclosure** is the process of taking possession of the mortgage property in satisfaction of a defaulting borrower's indebtedness and foreclosing a claim to any deficiency.
- **Power of Sale** is the process of taking the proceedings of the forced sale of a mortgage property in satisfaction of the indebtedness and in returning to the mortgager the excess over the indebtedness.

# Prior to accepting a mortgage
- Before an FI accepts a mortgage, it must satisfy itself regarding the property involved by doing the following:
	- Confirming the title and legal description of the property
	- Obtaining a survey certificate confirming that the house is within the property's boundaries
	- Checking with the tax office to confirm that no property taxes are unpaid
	- Requesting a land title search to determine that there are no other claims against the property
	- Obtaining an independent appraisal to confirm that the purchase price is in line with the market value

# Consumer (Individual) and Small Business Lending
- Techniques are very similar to those of mortgage lending.
- However, unlike mortgage loans, non-mortgage consumer loans focus on the individual's ability to repay rather than on the property.
- Credit scoring models put together more emphasis on personal characteristics.
- Small business scoring models often combine computer-based financial analysis of borrower financial statements with behavioral analysis of the business owner.

# Mid-Market Commercial and Industrial Lending
- Generally a profitable market for credit-granting financial institutions.
- Mid-market corporates are typically characterized as those having sales revenues from 5 million to 100 million per year and with a recognizable corporate structure.
- Commercial loans can be made for periods as short as a few weeks to as long as eight years or more.
- Short-term commercial loans are those with an original maturity of one year or less, and long-term loans are those with maturities of more than one year.

# Five Cs of Credit
1. **Character** refers to the probability that the loan applicant will try to honor the loan obligation.
2. **Capacity** is a subjective judgment regarding the applicant's ability to pay the FI according to the loan terms.
3. **Collateral** is represented by the assets that the loan applicant offers as security backing the loan.
4. **Conditions** refer to the general economic conditions or special developments that may affect the applicant's ability to meet loan obligations.
5. **Capital** is measured by the general financial condition of the applicant, as indicated by an analysis of the applicant's financial statements and leverage.

# Cash flow analysis
- As an initial step of the loan analysis, financial institutions require business loan applicants to provide cash flow information. Statement of cash flow separates cash flows into four categories or sections:
	1. Cash flows from operating activities
	2. Cash flows from investment activities
	3. Cash flows from financing activities
	4. Net change in cash and marketable securities

# Ratio Analysis
- This involves calculation of financial ratios and is useful when performing financial statement analysis on a mid-market applicant.
- **Liquidity ratios** express the variability of liquid resources relative to potential claims. 
- **Asset management ratios** give clues as to how well the applicant uses his assets relative to its past performance and the performance of the industry. 
- **Debt and solvency ratios** give an idea of the extent to which the applicant finances its assets via debt vs. equity.
- **Profitability ratios** express the profitability of the firm.
- Ratio analysis has limitations:
	- Many firms operate in more than one industry, which makes it difficult to construct a meaningful set of industry averages.
	- Different accounting practices can distort industry comparisons. 
	- It can be difficult to generalize whether a particular value for a ratio is good or bad.
