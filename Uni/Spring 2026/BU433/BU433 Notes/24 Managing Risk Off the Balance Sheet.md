# Introduction
- Managers are increasingly turning to off-balance sheet instruments such as forwards, futures, options, and swaps to hedge the risks their FIs face.
- As the use of derivatives has increased, so have the fees and revenues FIs have generated.
- Traders of derivatives can be speculators or hedgers.
	- Speculators buy derivative contracts to profit from a price increase or sell to profit from a price decrease.
	- Hedgers take a position in a derivative contract as protection against an increase or decrease in the price of a security.
- - -
# Forward and Futures Contracts
- A spot contract is an agreement to transact involving the **immediate exchange** of assets and funds.
- A forward contract is an agreement to transact involving the **future exchange** of a **set amount of assets** at a **set price**.
	- Participants take a position in forward contracts because the future price or interest rate on an asset is uncertain.
	- Buyer and seller must locate and deal directly with each other to set terms of contract.
	- Once a party has agreed to a forward position, canceling the deal prior to expiration is generally difficult.
- A futures contract is an agreement to transact involving the **future exchange** of a **set amount of assets** for a **price that is settled daily**.
	- It is very similar to a forward contract, with the difference between the two relating to price.
	- In a forward contract, the price is fixed over the life of the contract, whereas in a futures contract, it is marked to market daily.

# Hedging with forward contracts
- A naive hedge is a hedge of a cash asset on a direct dollar-for-dollar basis with the forward of a futures contract.
- Hedging allows financial institutions to immunize assets against interest rate risk.
- Though some hedging of interest rate risk does take place using forward contracts, many FIs hedge interest rate risk either at the macro level or at the micro level using futures contracts.
- **Microhedging** is using a derivative securities contract to hedge a specific asset or liability.
	- Number of futures contracts and FI should buy or sell depends on the interest rate risk exposure created by a particular asset or liability on the balance sheet.
	- Key is to take a position in the futures market to offset a loss on the balance sheet due to a move in interest rates, with a gain in the futures market.
	- **Basis risk** is a residual risk that occurs because the movement in a spot asset spot price is not perfectly correlated with the movement in the price of the asset delivered under futures or forward contracts.
		- Essentially, this means that the offsetting futures of the forward contract wouldn't completely balance the losses in the asset price.
- **Macrohedging** is hedging the entire duration gap of a financial institution.

# Options
- Financial institutions have a wide variety of option products to use in hedging, including the following:
	- Exchange-traded options
	- Over-the-counter options
	- Options embedded in securities
	- Caps, collars, and floors
- Four basic option strategies that FIs might employ to hedge interest rate risk:
	1. Buying a call option on a bond
		- Buying a call option is a strategy to take when interest rates are expected to fall.

	2. Writing a call option on a bond
	3. Buying a put option on a bond
	4. Writing a put option on a bond
