# Loan Sales and Securitization
- Loan sales and securitization are mechanisms that FIs have used to hedge their credit risk, interest risk, and liquidity risk exposures.
- Loan sales involve spreading out larger loans and loan portfolios, whereas loan securitization involves the grouping of smaller loans into larger pools.

# Loan Sales
- Loan sale occurs when an FI originates a loan and sells it with or without recourse to an outside buyer.
	- Recourse is the ability of a loan buyer to sell the loan back to the originator should it go bad.
- A large part of correspondent banking involves small FIs making large loans and selling (or **syndicating**) parts of the loans to large FIs, who in turn often sell parts of their loans (called **participations**) to smaller FIs.
- **Correspondent banking** is a relationship between a small bank and a large bank in which the large bank provides a number of deposit, lending, and other services.
- **Syndicated loan market** is the market for buying and selling loans once they have been originated. It can be segmented into three categories:
	1. **Market makers** are generally the large commercial banks that commit capital to create liquidity and take out right positions in the markets.
	2. **Active traders** are mainly investment banks, commercial banks, and culture funds.
	3. **Occasional participants** may be either sellers or buyers of loans.
- The syndicated loan market grew rapidly due to the expansion in **highly leveraged transaction (HLT) loans**.
	- HLT loans finance a merger and acquisition. A leveraged buyout results in a high leverage ratio for the borrower.
