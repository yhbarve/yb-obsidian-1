# Extra: Bank Of Canada's Shadow Banking Report
---
### Leverage
- It's the ratio between the total value and the value that's through your effort.
- In life, you always want to have a high leverage. This way, your able to maximize the output while being efficient and minimizing the effort required to produce that output.
- Similarly, banks would love to maximize their assets without having to use their money at all.
- However, higher leverage, higher is the risk. A slight drop in the prices, and you're facing massive loss and insolvency risk.
---
### Shadow Banking
- Consists of credit intermediation occurring partly outside the traditional regulated frameworks.
- Basically, these are banking activities that are done by companies or firms that are not banks.
- Because they are not traditional banks, they don't operate under the regular frameworks, which makes it difficult for the Bank of Canada to track these activities.
---
### Hybrid Monitoring Strategy
- **Entity-Based Monitoring**: Tracks leverage, maturity transformations, and liquidity variations directly on the balance sheets of non-bank firms.
- **Market-Based Monitoring**: Captures transactions, opaque or off-balance-sheet activities, and cross-sector interconnections, helping mitigate data collection limitations.
- **The Perimeter**: National housing instruments explicitly backed by the Canadian government (e.g., NHA MBS) and bank-owned investment dealers subject to consolidated bank supervision are excluded from shadow banking metrics. Conversely, non-bank dealers and certain investment funds are included.

### Core Subsectors
- The Canadian shadow banking industry is distributed across five main sub-sectors.
#### 1. Investment Funds (46%)
- Fixed Income Mutual Funds & ETFs: 
	- Form the largest segment.
	- Their vulnerabilities are limited by strict regulatory caps and leverage and conservative cash reserve requirements.
- Money Market Funds:
	- Have declined substantially, representing only 2% of total mutual fund assets under management, due to prolonged low interest rates and high competition from bank accounts.
- Credit Hedge and Credit Pooled Funds:
	- Carry strategies carry a low to moderate risk of runs.
	- Credit pools exhibit negligible leverage, while credit hedge funds have moderate median gross leverage of 2.9, with low overall exposure to illiquid securities.
#### 2. Repurchase Agreements & Securities Lending (29%)
- Vulnerabilities in the Canadian repo market are low, primarily because 74% of the underlying collateral consists of highly liquid Government of Canada (GoC) debt.
- The proportion of GoC debt used as repo collateral has been decreasing as a wider variety of less-liquid securities enter transactions.
#### 3. Non-Prudentially Regulated Lenders (11.4%)
- Finance Companies:
	- Exhibit low maturity transformations and maintain safe balance-sheet leverage, keeping total assets under four times their equity.
- Mortgage Finance Companies (MFCs):
	- Pass the vast majority of their credit risk to the regulated sector or government-backed securitizations
	- Only a small portion ($10 billion) of temporary "warehoused" mortgages are categorized as shadow banking.
- Mortgage Investment Corporations (MICs):
	- These entities handle residential mortgages but pose nominal systemic risk due to their small size and minimal leverage.
#### 4. Private-Label Securitization
- Dominated by credit card pools, auto-loans, and residential mortgages, this subsector has declined significantly from its 2007 peak of $178 billion.
- Higher post-crisis asset quality and strict regulatory boundaries have kept vulnerabilities low.
#### 5. Non-Bank Investment Dealers
- Driven by a lower commodity footprint and tight asset-to-equity caps managed under the Investment Industry Regulatory Organization of Canada (IIROC), these firms hold lower leverage (averaging 8) and robust liquid asset portfolios.

### Monitoring Challenges
- Opaqueness: A total lack of transactional visibility.
- Granularity: Limited Real-time Tracking Matrix
- Financial Innovation: Rapid technological innovations bypass conventional oversight frameworks and introduced unmapped liquidity parts requiring active and ongoing perimeter tracking
